Problems — Dividend & Yield

Problem 1 Dividend per share

To find the dividend per share, you need to divide the total dividend amount by the total number of shares. In this case, The Goose Man Corp declared a total dividend of ₱30,000,000.00, and there are 600,000.00 shares of common stock. So, you would divide ₱30,000,000.00 by 600,000.00, which equals ₱50.00. Therefore, the dividend per share is ₱50.00.

Problem 2 Cash dividend received

To calculate the dividend Ms. Yap received, you need to multiply the dividend percentage by the par value of the stock and then multiply by the number of shares she owns. The dividend percentage is 3%, the par value is ₱500.00, and she owns 200 shares. So, the calculation is 0.03 (3% as a decimal) times ₱500.00 times 200, which equals ₱3,000.00. Therefore, Ms. Yap received a dividend of ₱3,000.00.

Problem 3 Stock yield — Corporation A

For Corporation A, the stock yield ratio is calculated by dividing the dividend per share by the market value per share. Given a dividend per share of ₱7.00 and a market value of ₱56.00, the calculation is 7 divided by 56, which equals 0.125. Thus, the stock yield ratio for Corporation A is 0.125, or 12.5%. This indicates the return on investment based on the dividend relative to the stock's price.

Problem 4 Stock yield — Corporation B

To determine Corporation B's stock yield ratio, divide the dividend per share by the market value per share. With a dividend of ₱20.00 per share and a market value of ₱100.00, the calculation is 20 divided by 100, resulting in 0.2. Thus, the stock yield ratio for Corporation B is 0.2, which is equivalent to 20%. This percentage reflects the return on investment based on the dividend relative to the stock's market price.

Problem 5 Which is better?

Corporation B has a higher stock yield ratio than Corporation A. This means that for every peso invested in Corporation B, you would earn more in dividends compared to Corporation A. Therefore, if all other factors are the same, it would be a wiser financial decision to invest in Corporation B because it offers a higher return on investment based on the dividend yield.
